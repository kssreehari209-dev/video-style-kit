# Prompt to paste into Gemini (with the video attached)

You are an animation director and motion designer. Watch the attached video closely and write a style guide detailed enough that another artist could recreate the same look and motion without seeing the video.

Be specific and measurable. Give numbers, hex codes and timestamps wherever possible. If you are unsure, say "uncertain" rather than guessing.

Accuracy rules:
- Describe only what is visible or audible. If a detail is not visible in the video, write "not visible" instead of filling it in.
- Do not call something flat, outline-free or textureless unless you have checked for gradients, glows, particles, light rays and surface texture.
- Give a timestamp for every claim about a specific scene. Say which colors belong to which element (for example, the color of the brackets versus the color of the character).
- Never write "exactly" for a proportion you did not measure.

Cover every section below.

## 1. Overview
- Animation type (2D cel, vector/flat, motion graphics, 3D, stop motion, whiteboard, pixel, mixed)
- Frame rate (estimate), animating on ones/twos/threes
- Aspect ratio, resolution feel, total duration
- Overall mood and genre in one sentence

## 2. Color
- Main palette as hex codes (background, 3-6 primary colors, accent, shadow, highlight)
- Gradients, color grading, contrast, saturation
- How color changes across scenes

## 3. Line and shape
- Outlines or no outlines, line weight, line color, line texture
- Shape language (rounded, angular, geometric, organic)
- Level of detail

## 4. Shading and texture
- Flat, cel shading, soft gradients, grain, paper texture, halftone, glow
- Light direction and shadow style

## 5. Characters and objects
- Proportions, head-to-body ratio, eye and face style
- How characters are constructed (simple shapes, rigged parts, frame by frame)

## 6. Motion
- Easing (ease in/out, linear, bouncy, snappy) with typical durations in seconds
- Squash and stretch, overshoot, anticipation, follow-through
- Typical movement speed, loops, secondary motion

## 7. Camera and composition
- Shot types, camera moves (pan, zoom, parallax, shake)
- Layout rules, framing, use of negative space

## 8. Transitions and pacing
- Transition types between scenes (cut, wipe, morph, fade, match cut)
- Average shot length, rhythm, how text or elements enter and exit

## 9. Typography (if any)
- Font style or closest match, weights, sizes, text animation style

## 10. Sound (if any)
- Music genre and tempo, sound effects style, how audio syncs to motion

## 11. Timeline
A table of timestamps with what happens and which techniques appear.

## 12. Recreation recipe
- Step-by-step instructions to recreate this style
- A list of the 10 most important rules that define the look
- Common mistakes that would break the style

Finish with a compact summary prompt (under 200 words) that could be given to an image or video generator to produce this style.
